0. ONE-SENTENCE PITCH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Do not learn force from pixels.  Learn the *geometry* (a low-dimensional
deformation state q) from pixels, and let an interpretable, swappable linear
mechanics model turn q into contact location and force.  The learned part never
sees a force label and never needs retraining when the finger's material
changes; the physics part is exact, 8-dimensional, and runs in microseconds.


1. PROBLEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Compliant fingers make grasps forgiving, but the robot then has no idea how hard
it is squeezing or where it touches.  Tactile skins are expensive and fragile;
a camera is already on most robots.  Existing vision-to-force work regresses
force directly with a black-box network: it is opaque, needs force labels
(hard to get on soft bodies), and is tied to one specific finger.

Question: can a single fixed RGB camera give contact force *and* location on a
compliant finger, in closed loop, without markers on the finger, and without
learning anything about force?


2. KEY IDEA (contributions, ICLR framing)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(i)  Deformation as the learning target.  The finger's deformation field u ∈ R^{3N}
     lives on an 8-dimensional POD subspace (q = Φᵀu; 8 modes capture 99.99+ % of
     snapshot energy, held-out reconstruction 0.06 %).  q is a *geometric*
     quantity – it depends only on what the camera sees.

(ii) Markers as privileged information.  During training we render each state
     twice: with surface markers (a geometric least-squares estimator gives
     q_marker), and without.  A ResNet-18 is trained marker-free → q.  At test
     time no markers exist.  We show that supervising with the exact simulated q
     beats supervising with the marker teacher, and why (per-mode SNR).

(iii) Physics after the network.  A Galerkin ROM K_r = ΦᵀKΦ (8×8) turns q into
     force at a known contact by one scalar least squares, and into contact
     *location* by a residual search over the grasp face (~1000 candidates,
     O(C r)).  Same code for marker q and network q.

(iv) Generalisation for free.  Because the network outputs geometry, changing
     the finger material (E, ν) means rebuilding K only.  We sweep E ×0.25–×4
     and ν 0.30–0.45 with Φ, markers and network frozen: force is recovered to
     reference accuracy, and an un-updated model is wrong by exactly |E₀/E − 1|.

(v)  Closed loop.  A 7-DoF arm grasps and lifts an object with force feedback
     coming *only* from the fixed camera, both with markers and marker-free.

What is *not* claimed: no real-camera results; linear quasi-static elasticity;
one finger geometry; the contacting object is visible to the camera.


3. METHOD (short; the ROM is thin in the main text, POD details in appendix)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Mechanics.  Linear-elastic FEM of a clamped soft finger on a hex-dominant
quadratic mesh (20-node hexahedra, 10-node tetrahedra at the tip, hanging-node
ties; default element size = thickness/4 → 40×8×4 cells, 23.7 k DOF),
K(E, ν) u = B(c) λ n(c) for a normal point contact of magnitude λ at surface
point c.  Verified against Euler–Bernoulli / Timoshenko for the lateral (−y)
tip load used throughout (appendix A; top-face −z benchmark also available).

Reduced state.  Snapshots of unit-load solves on the grasp face → POD → Φ (r=8).
K_r = ΦᵀKΦ, B_r(c) = ΦᵀB(c).  Force at known c:  λ̂ = argmin_{λ≥0} ‖K_r⁻¹B_r(c)n λ − q‖.
Unknown c: precompute g(c) = K_r⁻¹B_r(c)n for a candidate grid, pick the c with
the smallest weighted residual (leading 4 modes), barycentric refine.

Two routes to q̂ (Fig. 1):
  D  markers   pixels of surface markers → LS with the image Jacobian of Φ → q̂.
  E  marker-free   240×180 RGB from the same world camera → ResNet-18 → q̂.
     Trained on 2 120 paired frames from the grasp scene (91 contacts × 10 forces
     × 2 poses + 300 free frames), target q_sim, 60 epochs, ~10 min on an M-series
     GPU.  Held-out contacts (31/91): q̂ error median 0.048 vs 0.27 for the marker
     estimator at this camera.

Temporal / control.  Kalman filter on q̂; before the contact lock, force is
computed at the nominal grasp point; after 5 consistent localisations within
15 mm the contact is locked and both ĉ and λ̂ feed a PI controller on gripper
opening; Coulomb lift check.

Material change.  Only K(E, ν) → K_r and g(c) are rebuilt; Φ is pinned to the
reference material (estimator.pod_reference_material) so q keeps its meaning and
the network checkpoint remains valid.

Self-weight.  The finger carries its own weight (ρ = 1200 kg/m³ → 0.24 N,
tip sag 0.35 mm = wL⁴/8EI; consistent quadratic body force, rotated into the
finger frame by the flange pose).  Linear ⇒ u = u_contact(λ) + u_g(R).  u_g is a *known load*: the
marker LS subtracts its marker motion in pixel space before the fit (exact –
u_g is a −z bending field, almost orthogonal to the −y press modes in Φ, so a
q-space correction Φᵀu_g would not remove it); on the hex-dominant mesh the
ROM force at 2 N reads 2.002 N with or without compensation (the former T4
mesh coupled y and z and read 1.918 N uncompensated).  The network regresses the
contact-only q and is empirically invariant to the sag in the image
(7.3 % force / 4.0 mm contact in the arm loop with the sag, 7.3 % / 2.8 mm
without) – no q correction for path E.  Ablation: uncompensated marker paths
degrade to 176 % (world camera) and 306 % (fixed camera), both drop the object.

5. LIMITATIONS (state them, reviewers will)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Synthetic renders only; no real camera, texture, specularity or sim-to-real.
* Linear, quasi-static, homogeneous isotropic elasticity; one finger geometry;
  point normal contact on one face.  Large-strain / hyperelastic fingers would
  need a nonlinear (or piecewise-linear) ROM – the q-then-physics split still
  applies, but K becomes K(q).
* Self-weight is included as a quasi-static body force (no inertia / dynamics);
  the marker-free checkpoint was trained on sag-free renders (it is invariant
  to the 0.35 mm sag in practice; the dataset generator renders the sag, so the
  retrained checkpoint has the invariance by design).
* The object is visible and acts as a location cue (Fig. 3 ablation: 1.2 → 7.6 mm
  without it – still 2.6× better than markers).
* Marker-free training data comes from the same simulator / camera family.
* The material sweep changes E and ν of a *known* material; unknown material
  would need identification (a natural follow-up: E from the ratio of commanded
  gripper displacement to observed q).

7. VENUE VARIANTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ICLR (primary, representation-learning angle)
  Lead with (ii)+(iv): a physically meaningful latent (q) as the learning target,
  privileged-information distillation, and *compositional* generalisation across
  materials because the learned module is decoupled from the physical parameter.
  Fig. 3 (why the teacher's high modes are noise → supervise with q, or SNR
  reweighting) and Fig. 5 are the core; Fig. 2 is the motivating demo.
  Reviewer asks to expect: real data (no), comparison with a direct image→force
  CNN baseline (add: train the same ResNet to output λ directly and show it
  breaks under material change – cheap to add with generate_grasp_nn_dataset.py),
  more modes / nonlinear.

ICML (methods angle)
  Same content; emphasise the structured latent + linear operator head as a
  general recipe (differentiable, closed form, 8×8), the per-mode SNR analysis,
  and the identifiability argument (E-only change is exactly a scale on q).

ICRA (robotics angle)
  Lead with Fig. 2 (closed-loop lift with a single fixed camera, marker-free),
  then Fig. 5 (change finger material, no retraining), Fig. 4 as the design
  guide (pixels / views / hold budget), Fig. 3 as the ablation.  Add timing
  (ResNet 1.5 ms, ROM 0.3 ms, 33 Hz loop) and the arm/IK details.  Real-robot
  experiment would be expected for a strong accept.
